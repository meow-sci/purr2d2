# Grounded somersault controls

The saber overlay adds forward, backward, left and right somersault buttons in a diamond below the swing control. Each circle shows a centered direction arrow. Each circle shares the slice button's radius and has a blue fill at 25% opacity (8-bit alpha 64). The buttons remain visible for the controlled EVA kitten across movement modes and terrain-contact gaps, including low-gravity running hops. Launch availability is separate from visibility: the first jump requires terrain contact; airborne follow-ups require the active combo window. Checkbox rows continue to control saber equipment independently.

Each deliberate press requests one jump. After the first launch is acknowledged, release and press any movement circle within an inclusive **0.75 seconds of the first accepted press** for a double jump. A third press within **0.75 seconds of the second accepted press** adds a triple jump. The interval defaults to 0.75 seconds and is adjustable from 0.1 to 1.5 seconds under **Settings → Character & grip**; changes save automatically. Three jumps is the maximum until landing. Every follow-up adds the first launch's upward speed to the current velocity, even while descending, without replacing horizontal motion or existing ascent/descent. It also adds one complete continuous visual rotation. The original direction stays latched throughout the combo, including when another direction's circle is pressed. Turns may overlap in time; neither contact timing nor the boost is tuned to guarantee an upright landing.

The window uses a monotonic, unscaled `Stopwatch` clock sampled when the press arrives and retained through the settled update and worker dispatch. KSA polls input after applying vehicle results but before the mod's settled update, so the input boundary first captures time and then observes the newly applied jump before checking follow-up eligibility. The draw-time availability check does not advance state. Render/worker delays cannot expire an already accepted press. One unacknowledged request is allowed at a time; duplicates cannot change its timestamp. Holding, native input repeat and dragging across a button cannot request another jump. Disabled circles capture clicks without deferring them. Hidden circles are click-through; an already captured release is still consumed. The layout reserves the same space when no controlled EVA is available.

The move works with or without a saber. Direction is relative to the kitten's heading at takeoff; rotating the camera during flight does not redirect it. Forward/backward are somersaults and left/right are airborne sideways flips. Standing flips target a 1.3 m apex above takeoff and approximately 1.5 m of level-ground travel. Moving flips retain at least the actual tangential takeoff speed in the selected direction. Between the game's walking and running speeds, the launch smoothly gains up to 10% extra travel speed and a 15% higher apex (1.495 m). A full stock 5 m/s run therefore launches at 5.5 m/s; moving flips travel farther instead of slowing to meet the standing hop's distance. Local gravity determines vertical launch speed and animation duration, so lower-gravity moves take longer. Slopes, drag and collisions can change the actual height, distance and landing time.

Starts require normal 1× simulation speed, RCS disabled, upright terrain contact and an active native physics bubble. The controls show a short availability reason when visible but disabled. A current saber swing or unfinished slice stroke must finish first. Beginning a somersault exits uncommitted slice selection; background cutting after a fully presented stroke continues normally. Saber click-and-drag remains available throughout the flip, with the hand pose applied before the full-body rotation. New canned swing and slice strokes are blocked during the move.

Saber ignition and equipment follow explicit player commands, independently of locomotion transitions. Grounded, airborne, MMU, tumbling and rightening modes retain the held saber. Ladder and swimming modes temporarily suspend the hand pose without clearing ignition or a pending equip; presentation resumes when a supported mode returns. Explicit retraction still takes effect during suspension, and boarding, world resets and disposal still release equipment.

## Physics ownership

`SomersaultMotionController` validates and patches the sole virtual `Vehicle.PrepareWorker(SimStep)` call in `Universe.PrepareVehicleWorkers`. Its wrapper calls the native preparation first, including derived EVA character/ladder snapshots, then modifies only the requested kitten's prepared worker state. It applies a bounded, surface-relative velocity delta once, takes the prepared body off rails, and initializes native airborne state without leaving a second stock jump impulse armed. Public vehicle state is updated through the game's normal solver results.

The queued request remains associated with its original kitten. Each launch/boost is consumed once in the final prepared worker and acknowledged through applied native jump timestamps before its visual turn advances. Follow-ups add only a current surface-up velocity delta and preserve the original native liftoff/contact history. KSA owns trajectory integration, gravity and collisions. There are no repeated position writes or intentional physical body rotations. Each turn contributes its own smooth angle on top of existing progress; accepting another turn never resets an already completed fraction. The pose adapter supports all three full turns.

Pose progress consumes applied simulation timestamps once and freezes while paused. Pause discards an undispatched extra jump and closes the combo until the next grounded jump; an already applied boost still acknowledges after resume without replaying. Landing follows native contact/state, not the estimated flight timer, and immediately closes follow-up eligibility. Takeoff's stale terrain contact does not close the window while native mode remains airborne. Early collision retains the original brief visual recovery toward the nearest upright turn, never unwinding completed turns. A longer fall holds all requested completed turns until real contact or timeout. This introduces no physical landing stabilization; native tumbling remains possible.

Movement and grab inputs are scoped to that kitten's prepared snapshot during the action. Held stock jump has a release latch to prevent a second automatic jump on landing. Control changes, boarding/disposal, world reload, editor entry and shutdown release owned state. Enabling RCS or unsupported time warp hands motion back to the game. Hiding the overlay or losing focus clears pointer state without cancelling an already launched flight.

The implementation refines the original research plan: its launch transaction modifies the final prepared worker snapshot, avoiding a split public-velocity mutation and a separate snapshot patch.

## Visual pose and optional tuck

`SomersaultPoseController` binds independently of saber equipment. It validates all skeleton roots and the model-to-body centre-of-mass conversion, then composes a procedural turn about that pivot. Rotation uses a continuous angle through a full turn; it does not interpolate between equivalent 0° and 360° quaternion endpoints.

Tuck is enabled only when recognized left/right hip-knee-ankle chains have valid hierarchy, distinct bones and plausible rest geometry. Supported rigs bend both legs and optionally the spine. Validated arm chains can fold into a carrying pose; the right arm remains under saber grip control when its saber is active. Unknown or incompatible leg rigs retain the full-body turn without tuck. No new model, texture or Blender asset is required for this implementation.

The ordered local pose is native animation → optional tuck → saber grip → full-body turn. Equip/unbind changes preserve that ordering. The game then reconstructs the final skeleton for each viewport, so the hand-mounted saber, helmet and other native attachments use the flipped pose. Main-view publication keeps the existing body-space freshness checks for hilt, contact, lights and audio. The visual turn never changes the physical collider or mass; the tuck does not let the kitten fit through smaller gaps.

## Validation and limits

Managed checks cover profile scaling with gravity, directional frame conversions, exact/expired/chained press windows, the three-jump limit, additive vertical boosts, preserved horizontal/frame velocity, delayed dispatch, once-only worker handoff, native launch/boost acknowledgement, held-jump behavior, pause and ownership changes, continuous double/triple turns and rendered pose angles beyond one turn, bone-length preservation, fallback rigs, explicit pose ordering, main/secondary view publication, responsive circular hit regions and release/press input capture. Host inspection validates the actual KSA 2026.10.19.5638 metadata and worker-call ordering. These checks do not execute the game's native terrain solver or render a real kitten.

In-game acceptance remains necessary on Earth and Luna: all four moves from standing and walking; double/triple jumps at each window boundary, mixed direction buttons and refused fourth/late presses; ascent and descent boosts; RCS rejection; held jump and repeated clicks; early contact, slopes, vessel impacts and tumbling landings; pause before/after a boost dispatch and resume; saber equipped/stowed and checkbox transitions; follow camera, portraits and secondary viewports; and switching kittens, boarding, save/load, disabling or unloading during a combo. Exact stock-rig tuck support and appearance must be observed in the game. Vessel-deck launches, swimming, ladder launches, unrelated free-flight flips and near-zero gravity are not supported.

Source investigation and alternative designs are recorded in [the research plan](research-somersault.md).
