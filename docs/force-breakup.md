# Furce expansion breakup

Outward Shift+scroll over Move Furce, while idle or dragging, builds continuous tension. Native GLFW scroll distance is normalized at 12 screen pixels per unit without rounding or counting events/detents, so fractional smooth-wheel and trackpad input accumulates normally. This remains independent of displayed spread, including at the configured ceiling. Maximum Furce spread defaults to 20× (2,000%) and is adjustable from 1–100× in global Furce settings. Displayed spread eases in and out with retained movement speed between wheel inputs; this smoothing does not change the raw tension input or delay breakup thresholds. Depth scrolling, compression and smoothing alone never arm breakup.

Two global settings control the thresholds: **Furce breakup groan (px)** defaults to **6 px**, and **Furce breakup split (px)** defaults to **18 px**. The first threshold plays the supplied `metal_creak.ogg` recording and starts preparation. The second requests breakup. The visible swap requires both ready replacements and at least **0.25 seconds of monotonic real time** since the first threshold was accepted. A single large input crossing both thresholds cannot bypass this delay. Time warp does not accelerate it, and a real frame hitch does not introduce extra waiting through a capped animation clock. The first visible commit plays the supplied `metal_bang.ogg` recording; neither cue loops or replays every frame.

Inward Shift+scroll unwinds tension. Falling below the split threshold withdraws the breakup request; falling below the groan threshold cancels preparation and rearms the groan. Release/throw, owner/world/topology changes, movement reset and threshold edits cancel pending work. Cancelled background work never joins on the game thread or publishes stale replacements into a later capture.

The controller validates the hold, snapshots authored visual nodes at full-part and subpart boundaries, checks the complete 512-object limit and prepares all native roots before committing any source. Main-thread capture uses precomputed primitive bounds rather than scanning every triangle. Immutable IDs, scalar bounds/transforms and dry masses go to a background worker for hashing, XML construction/deserialization, sibling overlap candidate calculations and persistent file writes. Native asset registration, collider cooking and detached Part/PartTree construction stay at joined handoffs, under a shared soft preparation budget of about **1.5 ms** with a bounded operation count. No incomplete task is synchronously waited on. A native operation is indivisible and may exceed that soft budget; the next unit waits for another handoff. Native vehicle creation and the final source swap still require the game thread, so large vehicles can take longer to prepare and need in-game frame-time checks.

**Settings → Furce settings → Breakup debris limit** controls the maximum new inert vehicles across the entire action. It defaults to **50**, accepts **2–512**, and saves automatically. The budget is captured when breakup preparation begins; changing it applies to the next breakup. Larger limits create more physics work. Main structural parts seed chunks first. If they already exceed the available budget, substantial parts become anchors and nearby whole parts join those chunks. Remaining capacity can peel substantial direct subpart branches, with deeper descendants kept together. Smaller trim and internal furnishings stay with a parent chunk. The limit is a maximum rather than a target piece count.

Every captured visible node belongs to exactly one output chunk. Compound templates preserve the original meshes, materials, glass and each node's position/rotation/scale; no geometry is truncated to fit the cap. Dynamic model surfaces become static wreckage. Invisible containers contribute their dry mass, and total dry mass is conserved across the chunks. Missing geometry and deployed parachutes still refuse preparation. Dents do not require mesh baking: capture copies each native deformation field and its source assembly transform. Every output chunk receives the whole fields whose dent radii overlap its bounds, including damage owned by another source part or by an invisible container. Fields remain separate on hidden, massless carrier subparts, so coarsening cannot merge them into KSA’s per-module dent limit or change their safety scaling. Initial transfer uses native `FxDeformation.ApplySaveData`, which populates both the private dent lists and simulation/render state. A construction hook recreates these modules on generated carriers during reload; ordinary native subpart save data preserves their dents. Carrier transforms live in the durable generated templates. Source dent changes invalidate pending preparation before commit.

For multiple captured vehicles, the controller selects sources in hold order, reserves at least two replacement chunks per selected source, and shares the configured quota while respecting the existing 512-object hold maximum. Sources that cannot fit remain intact and held. A completely full hold safely reports that more room is needed without starting a destructive action. Piece-count pressure coarsens or skips work; it does not reject a vessel merely because it contains hundreds of authored visual nodes.

Replacement templates contain rendering, picking, native mass/inertia and a centered box collider. They contain no engines, tanks, controls or seats. Their XML is append-only and shares the existing `Generated/Slices/` entry/disk limits and early startup reload. No new GPU mesh allocation is needed. Keep these files and any source generated slice assets with saves containing the debris.

Commit reads the source's current world pose and motion, so moving or rotating the hold during preparation cannot snap pieces back to an old position. It creates replacements as native orphan vehicles, depletes source consumables, stops its audio and calls native vehicle destruction. It never applies a part-failure event, explosion or random debris shedding. Crew follows native destruction, as with slicing. Conservative bounds reject separated siblings before allocating collision-avoidance pairs; overlapping siblings retain native suppression until clearance after release. Terrain and unrelated craft retain normal collision behavior.

The controller inverts the displayed group spread/translation to place the new pieces in the same hold frame. It preserves the original group center, untouched peers and pose target. Each committed source is replaced and its workers published immediately, so a later native failure cannot orphan earlier replacements. Release and throw use the existing controller paths. New Furce debris is a single physical object and does not recursively break apart on further expansion.

## Verification

Automated checks cover continuous threshold accumulation, early/late preparation, the minimum delay, inward cancellation/rearming, settings compatibility and ordering, one-shot audio/lifecycle, maximum-spread requests, owner/reset cancellation, mixed debris/clutter holds, replacement without position jumps after compression/translation, subsequent spacing/movement/throw, and recovery after a later commit fails. Budget checks cover large raw-node counts, per-action quotas across several vehicles, partial source selection and a full hold. Host checks deserialize compound XML with real KSA types and inspect grouping coverage, transforms/mass, native preparation, depletion/destruction and overlap protection. Dent regressions construct native parts and trees, transfer overlapping fields with more than ten total dents, check rotated/scaled/recentered field coordinates, serialize native vessel references to XML, and deserialize them through `PartTree.Deserialize`. They also cover damage owned by invisible nodes, subpart refinement, stale damage detection and cleanup of the global dented-part count. GPU/collider modules are omitted only from these managed save/reload fixtures.

In-game acceptance remains required:

- Capture a fueled multi-part vehicle; compress first, move it, then expand while hovering Move Furce. Confirm one metal creak at threshold 1 and one metal bang at the visible breakup, with at least 0.25 seconds between arming and the swap. Check every visible part/subpart stays held, with no explosion or missing geometry.
- Use small fractional trackpad/smooth-wheel motion, a single large scroll, inward unwind, and live threshold edits. Confirm buildup, cancellation and rearming; stage 1 alone must not split the craft. Move the hold while preparation runs and confirm the eventual swap uses its current position.
- Measure frame times on small and large craft during capture, background preparation, native preparation and the final swap; check no file I/O or task waits occur on the game thread.
- Break up the default vessel with hundreds of authored subparts. Confirm a useful part-first breakup capped at the configured limit (50 by default), with all hull/interior geometry still present. Check a multi-vehicle Angry Furce group shares the quota and leaves excess sources intact. Change the limit during preparation and confirm it applies only to the next breakup; restart and confirm the saved value returns.
- Repeat while the initial 1 m capture lift is still running, while dragging, at the configured spread ceiling, with rotating vehicles, and with Angry Furce holding additional vehicles and clutter. Confirm unchanged peers and paw aim do not jump. Raise the spread ceiling beyond 4× and lower it during a hold; confirm expansion past the old limit and a smooth return within the new limit. Check small fractional wheel input, rapid bursts and reversals for a gentle start, continuous motion and a settled endpoint without ringing.
- Release while siblings still overlap, release after they clear, throw, then recapture. Check sibling clearance, terrain contacts, debris motion and normal capture behavior.
- Save and reload the debris, including glass and scaled parts. Confirm durable templates resolve and old source vehicles/tank contents do not return.
- Dent several parts, capture the vessel and break it up at both low and high debris limits. Confirm the original dents remain on whole-part and peeled-subpart chunks, including where a field crosses a chunk boundary. Release/throw the debris, save, restart KSA and reload; confirm the dents persist and the generated carriers stay out of both editor catalogs. Keep `Generated/Slices/` with the save.
- Confirm preparation refusals for resource limits, unavailable geometry and deployed parachutes leave sources connected. Check occupied-vehicle destruction follows the same behavior as slicing.

## KSA 5801 atlas durability and slice offcuts

The 5801 native atlas loader registers generated MeshReference rows without calling their OnDataLoad. Their legacy IsReferenceable flag therefore remains false, even with a valid global ID and loaded primitive buffers. Breakup proves durability by resolving each mesh/material through the bound native registry and requiring object identity, successful loading and available geometry. Unregistered, replaced, incomplete or failed assets still refuse before source mutation; errors identify the source part/template, surface index and asset IDs. Host coverage loads the shipped practice atlas with real CPU geometry and reproduces the former refusal before accepting the registered surface.

Generated slice and Furce fragments already use native MarkAsDebris. Native connection branches detached while isolating a sliced part previously inherited the source's IsDebris=false and appeared as Vehicle in F11. After successful bulk tree finalization, slice isolation now marks only actual split participants without control modules, assigned crew, active control or editor ownership as debris. Partial separations receive the same treatment when bulk finalization succeeds; failed finalization and calls with no actual separation do not relabel anything. The native F11 collector and save flag are exercised in host tests. Existing unlabeled objects are not retroactively changed because older saves do not identify which native branches were cut off by this mod.
