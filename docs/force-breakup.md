# Force expansion breakup

Outward Shift+scroll over Move Force, while idle or dragging, builds continuous tension. Native GLFW scroll distance is normalized at 12 screen pixels per unit without rounding or counting events/detents, so fractional smooth-wheel and trackpad input accumulates normally. This remains independent of displayed spread, including at the 400% ceiling. Depth scrolling, compression and smoothing alone never arm breakup.

Two global settings control the thresholds: **Force breakup groan (px)** defaults to **6 px**, and **Force breakup split (px)** defaults to **18 px**. The first threshold plays the existing Light saber ignition recording as a placeholder groan and starts preparation. The second requests breakup. The visible swap requires both ready replacements and at least **0.25 seconds of monotonic real time** since the first threshold was accepted. A single large input crossing both thresholds cannot bypass this delay. Time warp does not accelerate it, and a real frame hitch does not introduce extra waiting through a capped animation clock. The first visible commit plays the existing saber retraction recording as the placeholder breakup sound; neither cue loops or replays every frame.

Inward Shift+scroll unwinds tension. Falling below the split threshold withdraws the breakup request; falling below the groan threshold cancels preparation and rearms the groan. Release/throw, owner/world/topology changes, movement reset and threshold edits cancel pending work. Cancelled background work never joins on the game thread or publishes stale replacements into a later capture.

The controller validates the hold, snapshots authored visual nodes at full-part and subpart boundaries, checks the complete 512-object limit and prepares all native roots before committing any source. Main-thread capture uses precomputed primitive bounds rather than scanning every triangle. Immutable IDs, scalar bounds/transforms and dry masses go to a background worker for hashing, XML construction/deserialization, sibling overlap candidate calculations and persistent file writes. Native asset registration, collider cooking and detached Part/PartTree construction stay at joined handoffs, under a shared soft preparation budget of about **1.5 ms** with a bounded operation count. No incomplete task is synchronously waited on. A native operation is indivisible and may exceed that soft budget; the next unit waits for another handoff. Native vehicle creation and the final source swap still require the game thread, so large vehicles can take longer to prepare and need in-game frame-time checks.

Each visual node becomes one inert vehicle. Existing meshes/materials are reused; dynamic model surfaces become static wreckage and glass keeps its rendering pass. Missing geometry, unbakeable dents and deployed parachutes refuse preparation. Invisible containers contribute dry mass to the visible pieces. Mass is distributed by authored volume within each part, with a small positive floor for decorative pieces; invisible-part mass is redistributed across the debris.

Replacement templates contain rendering, picking, native mass/inertia and a centered box collider. They contain no engines, tanks, controls or seats. Their XML is append-only and shares the existing `Generated/Slices/` entry/disk limits and early startup reload. No new GPU mesh allocation is needed. Keep these files and any source generated slice assets with saves containing the debris.

Commit reads the source's current world pose and motion, so moving or rotating the hold during preparation cannot snap pieces back to an old position. It creates replacements as native orphan vehicles, depletes source consumables, stops its audio and calls native vehicle destruction. It never applies a part-failure event, explosion or random debris shedding. Crew follows native destruction, as with slicing. Conservative bounds reject separated siblings before allocating collision-avoidance pairs; overlapping siblings retain native suppression until clearance after release. Terrain and unrelated craft retain normal collision behavior.

The controller inverts the displayed group spread/translation to place the new pieces in the same hold frame. It preserves the original group center, untouched peers and pose target. Each committed source is replaced and its workers published immediately, so a later native failure cannot orphan earlier replacements. Release and throw use the existing controller paths. New Force debris is a single physical object and does not recursively break apart on further expansion.

## Verification

Automated checks cover continuous threshold accumulation, early/late preparation, the minimum delay, inward cancellation/rearming, settings compatibility and ordering, one-shot audio/lifecycle, maximum-spread requests, owner/reset cancellation, mixed debris/clutter holds, replacement without position jumps after compression/translation, subsequent spacing/movement/throw, whole-group admission failure, and recovery after a later commit fails. Host checks deserialize generated XML with real KSA types and inspect native preparation, depletion/destruction and overlap-protection contracts.

In-game acceptance remains required:

- Capture a fueled multi-part vehicle; compress first, move it, then expand while hovering Move Force. Confirm one placeholder groan at threshold 1 and one placeholder retraction at the visible breakup, with at least 0.25 seconds between arming and the swap. Check every visible part/subpart stays held, with no explosion or missing geometry.
- Use small fractional trackpad/smooth-wheel motion, a single large scroll, inward unwind, and live threshold edits. Confirm buildup, cancellation and rearming; stage 1 alone must not split the craft. Move the hold while preparation runs and confirm the eventual swap uses its current position.
- Measure frame times on small and large craft during capture, background preparation, native preparation and the final swap; check no file I/O or task waits occur on the game thread.
- Repeat while dragging, at the spread ceiling, with rotating vehicles, and with Angry Force holding additional vehicles and clutter. Confirm unchanged peers and paw aim do not jump.
- Release while siblings still overlap, release after they clear, throw, then recapture. Check sibling clearance, terrain contacts, debris motion and normal capture behavior.
- Save and reload the debris, including glass and scaled parts. Confirm durable templates resolve and old source vehicles/tank contents do not return.
- Confirm preparation refusals for resource limits, unavailable geometry, dented surfaces and deployed parachutes leave sources connected. Check occupied-vehicle destruction follows the same behavior as slicing.
